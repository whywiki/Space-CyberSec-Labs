=== QUESTIONS ===

1. observability is different from logging because it combines metrics, logs and traces to give a full picture of system behavior, not just events - you can detect anomalies and correlate data across services, not just read what happened

2. metrics matter in mission ops because they show numerical trends over time - CPU load, telemetry throughput, service uptime - so operators can spot degradation before full failure

3. logs alone are not enough because they show individual events but not timing relationships between services or how one failure caused another; you can't reconstruct a full workflow from logs alone

4. traces show the full path of an operation across services - which components participated, how long each step took, where delays or failures occurred

5. a span is a single named operation within a trace - it has a start time, end time, and metadata; multiple spans form a trace representing a full workflow

6. OpenTelemetry correlates logs, metrics and traces using shared identifiers (trace ID, span ID) so you can link a slow metric to the exact log entry and trace that caused it

7. observability helps detect cyberattacks by making abnormal patterns visible - repeated commands from unknown users, unusual CPU spikes, unexpected service restarts all appear in metrics and logs and can trigger alerts

8. incident correlation matters in distributed systems because a failure in one service causes cascading effects elsewhere; without correlating telemetry across services you cannot tell which component failed first or what the blast radius was
